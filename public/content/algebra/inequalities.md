# Inequalities

## Introduction

An **inequality** is a mathematical statement that shows the relationship between two expressions using comparison symbols: $<$, $>$, $\leq$, $\geq$, or $\neq$.

Unlike equations (which assert equality), inequalities assert that one side is less than, greater than, or not equal to the other.

## Inequality Symbols

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| $<$ | Less than | $3 < 5$ |
| $>$ | Greater than | $7 > 2$ |
| $\leq$ | Less than or equal | $x \leq 4$ |
| $\geq$ | Greater than or equal | $x \geq 1$ |
| $\neq$ | Not equal | $x \neq 0$ |

## Solving Inequalities

The process for solving inequalities is almost identical to solving equations, **with one critical exception**: when you multiply or divide both sides by a **negative number**, you must **reverse the inequality sign**.

### Example 1: Addition/Subtraction (same as equations)

Solve: $x + 3 < 7$

$$x + 3 < 7$$
$$x < 7 - 3$$
$$x < 4$$

**Solution set:** $\{x \mid x < 4\}$

### Example 2: Multiplication by positive number

Solve: $2x \leq 10$

$$\frac{2x}{2} \leq \frac{10}{2}$$
$$x \leq 5$$

### Example 3: Multiplication by negative number (FLIP THE SIGN!)

Solve: $-3x > 9$

$$\frac{-3x}{-3} < \frac{9}{-3} \quad \text{(flip!)}.$$
$$x < -3$$

## Graphing Inequalities on a Number Line

To graph $x < 4$:
- Draw a number line
- Place an **open circle** at 4 (not filled, because 4 is not included)
- Shade **left** from the circle (all numbers less than 4)

To graph $x \geq -2$:
- Draw a **filled circle** at $-2$ (because $-2$ IS included)
- Shade **right** from the circle

## Compound Inequalities

A **compound inequality** combines two inequalities with "and" or "or".

### "And" (intersection)

$2 < x \leq 5$ means $x > 2$ **and** $x \leq 5$

The solution is the overlap: all numbers greater than 2 but less than or equal to 5.

### "Or" (union)

$x < -1$ **or** $x > 3$ means $x$ is either less than $-1$, or greater than $3$.

## Key Takeaways

- Solving inequalities follows the same steps as equations
- **Critical rule:** When multiplying or dividing by a negative, flip the inequality sign
- Compound inequalities with "and" require finding the intersection (overlap)
- Compound inequalities with "or" require finding the union (either/both)
- Always check your answer by substituting a test value
